Health for grades 6 to 12, and two consent-gated sexual health electives

- Pass HM: Healthy choices for grades 6 to 8 (health-6): your body and your health, food and movement, staying safe in person and online, and medicines, nicotine and other drugs, on the Texas grade 6 health standard and the National Health Education Standards, with four stories and a long one about Savanah's summer as a camp counselor, two Wonder questions, and Read the Label, a new kind of game. The reproductive and sexual health strand is left to districts and families, as Texas law sets out, and the crisis topics to school counselors.
- Pass HN: Health for life for grades 9 to 12 (health-9), five lessons on the Texas Health I course: health literacy and prevention, food and fitness, CPR and safety, mind and mood with 988, and drugs, medicine and choices; five stories and a long one about Mike and an AED at the ballpark, two Wonder questions, and Push to the Beat, a new kind of game.
- Pass HO: Growing up healthy (sexual-health-6), an elective for grades 6 to 8 that appears only when an educator assigns it, on the reproductive and sexual health strand of the grade 6 health standard: puberty, reproduction and pregnancy, healthy relationships, and safety, reporting and abstinence; four stories and a long one about Savanah and a fetal heart at twenty weeks, two Wonder questions, and Green Flag or Red Flag, a new kind of game. Assigning it asks the educator to confirm a parent's written consent, as Texas Education Code §28.004 requires of schools, and its Wonder questions never reach a student who was not assigned it.
- Pass HP: Relationships and health (sexual-health-9), the grades 9 to 12 human sexuality elective, consent-gated like the grade 6 course: healthy relationships and boundaries, safety, abuse and trafficking, STIs, testing and prenatal care, and choices, risks and responsibilities, with four stories, a long story about Georgette and a question box, two Wonder questions, and Myth or Fact, a new kind of game.
- Pass HQ: the intermittent refresh test failure traced to its real cause, a reload that sometimes started before Chromium had committed the page's localStorage writes; the test now carries the store across the reload, and the earlier wrong explanations are corrected in DECISIONS.
