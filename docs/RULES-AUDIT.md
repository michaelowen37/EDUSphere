Nothing here is deleted or changed without Mikey's approval first; a rule that softens or moves gets its row edited, with the date, only after he has said so.

How to read the **Enforced** column: **test** means a test in tests/ fails the build when the rule is broken (the strongest kind; check.sh runs them all before every delivery); **tool** means a report in tools/ shows the breach and the delivery compares its number with the last delivery's (strong, but a person must read the number); **doc** means the rule is written here or in CLAUDE.md, NEW-CHAT.md or FABLE-REVIEW.md and applied by hand (the weakest kind; it holds only while a chat reads and keeps the doc); **none** means it lived in Mikey's head and the chat history until it was written here. ## 1. The vision (docs/VISION.md, kept here in full because every change is judged by it)

The problem: school takes six hours a day and most of it is not learning. Children who fall behind were moved on before something stuck, and everything built on it wobbled. The bet: two hours of focused, adaptive practice a day covers the academic ground better, because nobody is pushed forward until the thing before is solid, and the other hours become the point of childhood. The Wise Human is that idea for a homeschooling family or a microschool, on an iPad, with no account, no server, and no child's data leaving the room.

What the product must always do:
- **Master, then move.** A module opens only when everything it needs is mastered. Mastery is measured in one sitting, never patched together across days.
- **Go back when needed.** Repeated misses send the student back to the prerequisite, even one that looked solid. A gap further back is usually the real problem.
- **Bring it back.** Old material returns inside later rounds. If it did not stick, we find out, and the educator sees it.
- **Never invent.** Every question is generated by arithmetic and checked by an independent test. No AI writes questions at question time.
- **Say it in words.** Educators read sentences, not tables. Children who cannot read get pictures, sound and one thing to tap.
- **Keep nothing we do not need.** A school-issued ID, what was assigned, what happened in practice. Nothing a child writes. No names, photos, or trackers.
- **Reflect often, and for a reason.** Reflection questions come after every second finished round, pass or fail, each written toward one of four aims: curiosity about the world; failure as the way learning happens; naming and handling feelings; and the truth that hard days are a normal part of being human. After a failed round the failure and feelings questions come first. No question is offered a third time. The pool grows with the curriculum, and a check says so when it falls behind.
- **Stay neutral.** Four voices and none is right. Nothing argues for or against religion. Nothing reaches a child until the school approves it.

What it is not: not a replacement for a teacher, and not a claim. We do not say students outperform anyone until real classrooms show it. Who it is for, in order: microschools and homeschooling families; families supplementing a school; daycare centers with the pre-K material; later, trades and workplace training with the child-facing parts switched off. How to tell if a change is right: does it help a child master something, help an educator see clearly what happened, or free up time for real life? If it does none of those, it does not belong. If it stores something about a child we did not need, it does not belong. If it lets a student past something unmastered, it is wrong.

Accuracy above everything (Mikey, every pass): one wrong fact can ruin the product's reputation. Standards are read in full and quoted word for word, never paraphrased, even when that costs time. Newest research; older findings as history with dates; statistics sourceable; nothing a learner has to unlearn later.

## 2. The product, as it stands (walked in pass LD; update this map when a screen changes)

- **Welcome:** "Who's learning today?" with the students' pictures; Educator Login; Create account; everything lives on this device.
- **Educator setup:** a four-to-six-digit PIN (recoverable only with a backup), the device's name (part of the backup file name), the state (Texas first; the plan maps TEKS and Common Core).
- **My Classroom:** add a student by school-issued ID, choose a level (Early years pre-K to grade 2; Elementary 3 to 5; Middle school 6 to 8; High school and beyond), a sign-in picture with a colour, an optional four-digit student PIN, Wonder on or off; rename, change picture, hide, merge; open the report. Quick checks on or off. Walk through as a student (every module open, nothing recorded). Wonder questions to review (nothing reaches a child until approved). Reading lists, science experiments, practical life skills, backup, contact.
- **Placement (the truth as of pass LE, Mikey):** a student added below grade 3 gets no placement check, so the screens stay simple; a student added at grade 3 or above gets a placement check per subject that places them one notch conservatively; quick checks then let a student skip individual modules. When an early-years student is added, the grade picker says there is no placement check and, once a grade is chosen, a one-line note of what a child starting there should already be comfortable with (pass LE).
- **Student report:** notes (kept with the student, restorable, searchable from Who Needs Help); a summary in sentences (what is mastered per course, confidence, reflections); assigned courses with the recommended set and a way back to it; progress by course; other courses folded by grade and by core and electives; print.
- **Student side:** the overview of courses with modules; a lesson (key idea, paragraphs or a spoken script with a picture per line, example, story-based learning fold, pictures to come); practice (a round of five plus memory checks; pre-readers retry a wrong answer, "Not that one. Have another try."; older students see the explanation); results and stars; reflections (Wonder) after every second round; the course's long story and game when finished; Let's Color and Let's Play folds; certificates.
- **Not built yet:** weekly or daily educator reports and recommendations (an earmarked idea); the fourth exposure's screens (its logic is built, pass LF; approved in pass LE, built next); long stories for twenty early courses (L1).

## 4. Writing: Eleven v4 audio

What is written and checked today: the audio ledger (docs/AUDIO-LEDGER.md and .csv) lists every clip with its key, group and tagged words; every clip stays far under Eleven v4's 10,000-character request (longest clip 508 characters, longest story 3,420); tools/audio-generate.mjs sends each clip with the words before and after (previous_text, next_text) so a story sounds like one telling, skips clips already made, and saves audio/<key>.mp3; the age band carries a voice direction; the app plays recorded clips once every clip of a story or lesson line exists and keeps the device voice until then.

**Eleven's own rules and examples, recorded from Mikey's screenshots of the ElevenLabs documentation (pass LE), live in docs/ELEVEN-V4-AUDIO-TAGS.md, which every chat reads before tagging a story.** The short form: a tag is a natural-language cue in square brackets, placed before the words it affects; its emotion carries forward across the line, so a new tag goes only where the delivery shifts (one to three a paragraph, at its turns); tags combine with commas inside one bracket ([whispering, playful]); punctuation shapes pacing alongside the tag; the kinds are emotion, delivery and volume ([whispers], [shouts], [softly], [quietly]), pacing ([slowly], [rushed], [pause], [long pause], [drawn out]), human-like reactions ([laughs], [sighs], [gasps], [clears throat], [crying]), accent and character ([British accent], [pirate voice]) and sound effects ([thunder rumbling], [footsteps], [door creaking]); a tag can be written as a fuller direction (a manner, a situation, a character, a shift); SSML is disabled in v4, so [pause] and [long pause] are the breaks; pronunciation dictionaries define how names are spoken; a single generation takes up to 10,000 characters and context stitching keeps delivery steady across generations, which the generator already uses, so no story is ever shortened for the voice. Ours on top: the closing moral is [slowly, warmly]; sound effects are short phrases naming a sound the story names, one to three a story; the audio array is the words with the tags inserted and nothing else changed (test). The 503 tags already in the stories fit these rules (the most used: [slowly, warmly] 177, [proud], [delighted], [thoughtful], [curious], [gently], [puzzled], [pleased], [playful]); 177 of 187 tagged stories close with [slowly, warmly], and the ten that do not are read in the next review.

### 7a. The Quick Review (the fourth exposure, called the quick look back until pass LP), Practice Reviews and Action Items (Mikey, passes LE to LP)

**Pass LP, from Mikey's notes on the screenshots.** The Action Item popups center their title and text, with "1 of 2" stacked under the title, papers too. The student side is named "Quick Review" everywhere: the start-of-day card "First, A Quick Review" (or "One More Quick Review" if the student left after the first of two), its modules stacked one above the other, the early learners' speaker icon instead of a Hear it button, an × to exit; the questions headed "Quick Review" over "Question 1 of 5"; the result page titled "Quick Review", the module's name in bold in the line, and "Next" for a second review. The report's Practice Reviews card lists every review, every Move Forward and a lesson stuck right now as its own entry with a small date stamp above it, newest first (lightReviewHistory); two show, and a History link, there only when there are more than two, opens the rest. Reviews are kept for good, so the card stays once it appears. The weekly note carries that week's entries under "Practice reviews:", so one printed note holds everything; Who Needs Help lists a lesson stuck after its review (weighted like any stuck lesson) and a lesson a review reopened (weighted like one miss).

Every rule of this feature in one place, so a chat reads it whole. Code: src/logic.mjs (lightReviewState, lightReviewQueue, lightReviewDue, buildLightReview, makeLightReviewEvent, lightReviewOutcome, reviewingIds, probeIds, lightReviewStuck, stuckAfterReviewIds, makeMovedForwardEvent, stuckSentence, lightReviewSentences) and src/ui.jsx (startLightReview, the start-of-day card, the light-review-result screen, the Practice Reviews card, ActionCard, ActionItemPopup, ActionWritingPopup, actionCount, SampleClassroomCards). The sentences below are copied from the code's own output, so they are exactly what a screen shows (asterisks mark a title shown in bold).

**What it is.** A lesson is met four times: the lesson, the practice that masters it, the memory checks inside later rounds, and this short review weeks later, which decides whether the lesson is worth teaching again. Students see it as a "Quick Review" (Mikey, pass LP); educators see it as "Practice Reviews".

**When one comes due.**
- 21 calendar days after the lesson's star (LIGHT_REVIEW_FIRST_DAYS). Calendar days, because forgetting runs on the calendar whether or not the student logs in; Mikey chose calendar days over days logged in (pass LK).
- At most two a day per student, by the device's calendar day (LIGHT_REVIEWS_PER_DAY = 2). Every review that has come due waits in a queue, oldest due first (lightReviewQueue). When two or more are due, the first two are offered together at the start of the day and taken back to back, and a third waits for the next day (Mikey, pass LM: no waiting for a week's worth first, so the queue does not back up; passes LK and LL had waited). Two a day keeps pace with a student who masters up to two lessons a day; a student who masters more for weeks on end builds a slow queue that the reviews work through two a day. A review is due on its day: it is offered from the start of that day, not from the hour its three weeks run out, so a review never appears in the middle of a student's lessons (pass LM).
- Only lessons in courses the student can see are queued, so a course the educator switched off holds nothing up. A day the student does not open the app costs nothing; the queue waits.

**How the student meets it.**
- When one is due it opens the student's day: the overview shows only a card, "First, A Quick Review", naming the lesson ("Five quick review questions from a module you've mastered long ago. Your lessons come right after!", with the module named below in a pill; two modules stack one above the other), with Start, the speaker icon for an early learner only, and an × to exit. It never starts by itself, because a child should see what is coming first. The lessons appear as soon as it is done. When two are due the card shows both from the start, headed "First, A Quick Review", with both lessons named in order; Start begins the first, its result screen offers "Next" straight into the second, and the lessons follow when both are done (Mikey, pass LL: back to back, never spread between lessons). A student who leaves after the first finds "One More Quick Review" on returning.
- Five questions from the lesson's own bank, no question twice; a retry opens with the kinds of question missed last time. It runs on the practice screen like any round, headed "Quick Review" over "Question 1 of 5", spoken on a read-aloud course.
- The result screen never uses the word fail (Mikey's wording). A clear pass: "You remembered Teen Numbers well. That one is yours to keep!" A bare pass: "You remembered a lot of Teen Numbers. We will look back at it once more soon." A fail: "Teen Numbers is worth another visit. It's open again on your list so you can go back through it any time." Spoken on a pre-reader's course.
- A lesson reopened by a failed review carries a line on the overview: "You've mastered this once before but one of your reviews prompted a refresher. Go through it once more, story and all, when you're ready." (Mikey, pass LP)

**The three outcomes.**
- Four or five right is a clear pass, and the schedule ends for that lesson.
- Three right is a bare pass: another review 7 days later (LIGHT_REVIEW_RETRY_DAYS), opening with the kinds of question missed.
- Two or fewer is a fail: the lesson reopens for a full round with its story, the memory checks in the next rounds favor the lessons it builds on (probeIds, buildAttempt's probe option), and passing it again earns a new star that restarts the schedule.
- Nothing is ever rewritten. A star stays a star, and every review is one appended light_review_completed event.

**When a student is stuck.**
- The app works on it alone first. A lesson is stuck only when, after its failed review, the student has missed it twice, been looped back to the lesson before it, and missed it twice more with no pass in between (lightReviewStuck). Until then the educator is not asked.
- There is no educator switch for reviews (Mikey, pass LF): they help, and the student card has enough on it.
- Move Forward records one moved_forward event. The review closes for that lesson; the star, the gate and the memory checks stand, so the lesson keeps coming back lightly inside later rounds and a later slip still shows. A new star restarts the schedule.

**What the educator sees.** Mikey's style for every sentence: explanatory, varied sentence lengths, context, no jargon or assumed knowledge; titles in title case.
- The Action Item link: bold red pulsing text on My Classroom, centered underneath the Wonder Questions link and its On pill on every device (Mikey, pass LL), shown only when something needs the educator.
