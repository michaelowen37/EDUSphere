## The brief

You are illustrating EduSphere, a children's learning app for pre-K through high school. Every image follows one template and one look, so a math picture and a history picture sit side by side as one family (one exception, Mikey's answer to Q33 in pass MI: the four Wonder portraits, U2 to U5, are flat and outlined, each the same person as its 3D character sheet, since they show small beside line-art pictures):

`[Core Subject Concept], [Playful Modifier]. Style: Clean 3D vector illustration, vibrant and engaging color palette, minimalist [scene] background, balanced lighting. Professional educational graphic style, clear focal point, uncluttered layout, [Visual Anchor].`

The modifier and the anchor change with the subject, and nothing else does:

| Subject | Playful modifier | Visual anchor and composition | Background |
|---|---|---|---|
| Science | wonder-filled and vibrant, stylized, curious | dynamic close-up, glowing elements, soft shadows | minimalist lab or field background |
| History | friendly and whimsical yet respectful, stylized historical accuracy | heroic composition, soft cinematic lighting, simple period elements | minimalist period background |
| Math | cheerful and neat, colorful, geometric | distinct geometric shapes, high-contrast axes, no text labels | minimalist solid background |
| Reading and Writing | imaginative and expressive, storybook style | character-focused, dreamy lighting, central framing | minimalist library or storybook background |
| Art, Music, Technology, Health | as Reading for people, as Science for things | character-focused or dynamic close-up | minimalist background |

Sample prompts in this voice: a baking soda volcano erupting with colorful foam, wonder-filled and vibrant, minimalist lab background, dynamic close-up. George Washington crossing the Delaware, friendly and whimsical yet respectful, minimalist river background, heroic composition. A 3D pie chart of a pizza cut into parts, cheerful and neat, minimalist solid background, distinct geometric shapes. A friendly owl in oversized glasses reading a giant glowing book, imaginative and expressive, minimalist library background, character-focused.

Rules that never change:
1. No text in the image (no words, letters, labels, numbers, signs or logos), with one exception since pass ML (Mikey's answer to Q42: "relax it for scene words only, from grade 3 up."): a story scene, a long story's scene or a lesson painting for grade 3 or older may carry one or two short words that belong to its scene (a street sign, a shop's name, a book's cover, a jersey), written in the prompt in double quotes the way Leonardo's prompt guide asks (exact, under about 25 characters, the lettering described in general terms, such as bold, plain capital letters), spelled out in the row's check note and read letter by letter before the painting is saved, so a wrong letter costs only a retry. A word that teaches or that a question asks for (a letter in a reading lesson, a numeral, a count, a clock, a label on a diagram, a map's names) is drawn by the app, never painted, and pre-K to grade 2 keep the rule whole, since their lessons teach letters by their shapes to children learning to read; coloring pages, character sheets, the Wonder portraits and the maps stay wordless. Anything else that needs writing (a periodic table, a chart, a clock face, a map's names) is drawn by the app, not by you. Every ledger row carries its bans: `text, words, letters, labels, numbers, logos, watermark, signature, blurry, extra fingers, extra limbs, deformed hands, photorealistic skin`, and a scene-word row says `text other than the scene words` in place of the first three. The Nano Banana models have no negative prompt (Leonardo's pages for them list none), so since pass MC (Mikey: "Yeah, let's fold into the prompt.") docs/LEONARDO-PROMPTS.csv folds each row's bans into the prompt as closing sentences that say what the picture is, the way Google's guide for the model advises ("Describe what you want, not what you don't want"): the picture is wordless, telling everything through shapes and color (a picture card or a poster still shows its drawing; a scene-word row says "The only writing in the picture is "CASH TODAY", spelled exactly so in large, plain letters." and keeps the rest wordless), hands have five fingers where a person is drawn. Paste the spreadsheet's prompt whole. A row whose status reads Re-plan first (A6) asks for drawn writing its bans forbid; leave it until it is re-planned.
2. One model for the whole set: Nano Banana 2.1 in Leonardo since pass MK (Mikey: "there is now a Nano Banana 2.1 so that is the one I would use over Nano Banana 2"; Nano Banana 2 from pass MC; the brief named Vision XL or Diffusion XL before that), style preset None, since the template sets the look in words, and Prompt Enhance off, so the checked words reach the model as written. The same aspect ratio for story scenes (4:3), minimalist or solid backgrounds, one clear focal point. Never switch models between subjects; the template gives the variety. The one exception since pass ML (Mikey's answer to Q44: "run the trial, then move them if they pass"): the coloring pages, outlines on white that share no look with the paintings, move to Lucid Origin and are painted last, once the five-page trial at the top of the spreadsheet passes (The coloring trial, below).
3. Keep backgrounds minimalist so the eye lands on the concept. Wholesome always: kind faces, warm light, nothing frightening, no violence shown even in history scenes (show the walls of the Alamo, not the battle; show the torn map, not the field).
4. Recurring characters must be recognizable across years. Each has a character sheet (C serial). When a scene names a sheet, attach that sheet as an Image Reference at High (Character Reference is a feature of Leonardo's older models, not of Nano Banana 2.1), keep the face, eye color, skin tone, hair color and build exactly, and change only what the scene asks for (age, clothes, setting). Never redesign a character to fit a scene.
5. Flags. A national, state or historic flag is left out where the picture can do without it. Where it cannot, aim for an accurate one, and, as Mikey put it (pass LZ), "perhaps it can be waving in the wind or folded slightly to purposely obscure the pattern a little": outdoors it may wave and indoors hang in soft folds, hiding part of its pattern. Its stars or its band sit by the pole, and a hand over the heart is the right hand. A simple flag a lesson teaches whole (the Texas flag) may show whole. A picture that would be better with an exact flag, or with words a picture usually cannot carry, has its hoped-for version under Images to try at the end of docs/ART-REQUESTS.md: try it once or twice, save it under the same serial if it passes its check, and otherwise paint the planned version.

## How to keep them consistent, step by step

1. Generate the character sheets first (C1 to C14 in docs/ART-REQUESTS.md, made as The reference set above says): front and side view, neutral pose, plain background. Iterate until the face is right, then lock that image: it is the reference for everything after.
2. For every scene whose row names a reference in the spreadsheet's references column, attach that reference as an image reference at High strength and, for the set's look, one approved scene as a second reference (Nano Banana 2.1 takes up to six). The four Wonder portraits (U2 to U5) take their sheet alone, since an approved 3D scene would pull them back to 3D; once one portrait passes, attach it as the second reference for the other three. The column stays empty on a picture with no one in it, even in a character's story (a jar of soil in Chloe's), because an attached sheet would put the character there; the same holds for a picture whose only person is someone else (CS473's intern; EARMARKS A22 lists the rows where the spreadsheet still names a sheet, so check that the sheet's character is in the scene before attaching it), and a picture of the character writes out the age and clothes of that story's step, since the sheet paints the age it was drawn at. If the panel offers a seed (the screenshots show none), keep the seed of a good result and reuse it when re-rolling small changes.
3. Aging a character: start from the previous chapter's sheet as the reference and describe the change as an addition ("the same face, now with a close dark beard and no hair"), never as a new description. Save the result as the next sheet.
4. Leonardo's custom Elements (a trained model of a character) run on its own models, Flux Dev in its model guide, not on Nano Banana 2.1, so an Element per core character would mean a second model for the cast; weigh it on generation day (EARMARKS L14) against rule 2's one model.
5. Name every export by its serial (S14.png), and upload it with the serial. The app stores it as art/stories/S14.webp. A screen picture (U serial) goes to the same folder, painted square (1:1): since pass MH U2 to U5, the Wonder voices' portraits, flat and outlined since pass MI, which show as a circle 96 pixels wide (64 on a reader's card); U1, the Surprise me row's question marks, was retired in pass MH (Mikey: "I actually like the question marks you drew for the surprise me section.
