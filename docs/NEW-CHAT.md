## How to start a new chat

1. Take the last delivered zip into the repository folder with tools/update-from-zip.sh (docs/RUNBOOK.md) and commit it on GitHub, using docs/COMMIT-MESSAGE.md, so the new chat starts with a fresh commit message.
2. Open a new chat inside the claude.ai Project "The Wise Human".
3. Upload the latest delivered edusphere-project.zip. A new chat starts with an empty workspace; the zip is the repository. If the last chat stopped in the middle of a pass, upload its last edusphere-checkpoint-<label>.zip instead (see Checkpoints below).
4. Send: "New chat for The Wise Human. Unzip the project, read docs/NEW-CHAT.md, docs/RULES-AUDIT.md, docs/EARMARKS.md, docs/INDEPENDENT-CHECKS.md and the latest DECISIONS entry, then continue. Committed through <pass>." For a checkpoint zip, add: "This is a checkpoint; read CHECKPOINT/CHECKPOINT.md and carry on from where it stopped."
5. Answer any open question by its number ("Q67. (b).") in the same message; the numbers are in Questions for Mikey below.
6. Keep the Project's knowledge current: docs/PROJECT-CONTEXT.md, titled Project Context, is the document the claude.ai Project holds for every chat (pass MM, at Mikey's request); a delivery message says when it changed and should be uploaded again. The Project's older copies of New Chat, Claude MD and Multi-Agent Checks are out of date and can be removed, since the zip carries the current files.
7. When Mikey says only "continue", the last pass is uncommitted; he says when he commits. Confirm with him what is committed, and start the commit message fresh after each commit he reports. (This live rule sat inside the history bullet of passes IR to JB until pass MM moved it here.)

Whether the youngest lessons invite a child to say a new word aloud (pass MQ, the standards review: Domain II's vocabulary outcomes ask that a child "understands (receptive) and uses (expressive)" words, and pass MQ's four vocabulary lessons, Learning words, People and events, More learning words and Words that go together, teach the understanding half, since the app never listens, so the using half is left to the child's own talk). Where: the spoken lessons of pre-K 3 and pre-K 4 (Very First Steps, Listen and Point, First Steps, and First Sounds and Letters), for three- and four-year-olds, wherever a lesson brings in a new word, such as grandpa, sort or opposite. Example: "This is a grandpa. Say it with me. Grandpa!" and then a short pause, about two seconds, before the next line. Claude recommends (b): it is what the research did, it costs little, and you can hear how it feels in a lesson before it spreads.

## Checkpoints

A pass runs for hours, and a chat can stop in the middle (a usage limit, a lost turn), so since pass MD (Mikey: "Can you create checkpoints more often so we don't lose as much work when this happens? Maybe they can be mid-cycle dandruff documents that can be re- uploaded into the chat.", handoff documents, as he meant) a pass sends Mikey a checkpoint zip at each milestone: after the content or code is built and the fast checks pass, after the first round of independent checks is applied, after the second reads, after the docs, and whenever two hours of work have gone by without one. `bash tools/checkpoint.sh <label> <out dir> <scratch folders...>` makes it: the whole project as it stands plus a CHECKPOINT folder, where CHECKPOINT/CHECKPOINT.md says which step of the pass is done, what comes next and what Mikey has been told, and CHECKPOINT/work holds the pass's reports, patch scripts and diff packets from the scratchpad. The script refuses a CHECKPOINT.md older than two hours. The memory checkpoint is kept beside it, as before. To resume, a new chat uploads the checkpoint zip, unzips it, reads CHECKPOINT/CHECKPOINT.md and carries on; before the pass is delivered, `bash tools/checkpoint.sh --clean` removes the folder, the delivery zip's exclusions leave `CHECKPOINT/*` out (tools/changes-zip.sh and the zip command in How a pass is delivered alike), and tools/update-from-zip.sh refuses a zip that carries the folder (tests/update-from-zip.test.mjs holds it), so a checkpoint can never reach the repository by mistake. Nothing in a checkpoint is a delivery: the checks may not have run, and the docs may be half written.

## The vision

The Wise Human (formerly EduSphere), by iECHO, LLC, tagline "Where Knowledge Meets Wisdom": a mastery-based, adaptive learning app from pre-K 3 through college that teaches understanding and wisdom, not memorization. Students learn through lessons that demonstrate, stories that make ideas stick, Wonder questions that build judgment and resilience, games, and memory checks that keep knowledge alive. docs/VISION.md holds the longer statement.

Since pass MM, Mikey's words: "We don't need to lean too heavily in the 2 hour learning part of the vision; depth, quality of learning and student experience matter more." The two hours stay the bet, but a change is judged first by what it does for depth, the quality of the learning and the student's experience; time saved is a gain only when none of those pays for it.

- Audience: homeschool families and microschools, Texas first, through the Texas Education Freedom Accounts vendor plan (docs/TEFA-PLAN.md).
- The moat is depth: every subject strand from kindergarten to grade 12 and college, standards alignment checked against published texts, a recurring cast that grows up with the learner, Wonder questions, a new game for every course, large question banks and an offline, private design. A few lessons are easy to copy; the whole connected system is not.

## The two rules above all others

1. Accuracy of absolutely everything, whatever it costs in time or passes. Accuracy of what already exists comes before new material (Mikey, pass JZ). Standards are quoted only from published texts read online in full before citing. Facts, dates and figures are checked twice; numbers are computed in code wherever possible. Uncertainty is stated, never guessed over. Mistakes are owned, fixed and recorded in docs/DECISIONS.md.
2. Educational quality and the learner's flow come first. When a house rule would make the learning or the experience worse, do the right thing for the learner, say so plainly, and record the exception. Mikey's example: the youngest two-way modules grew past five different questions because more examples teach better. Adding questions, examples or variety is welcome whenever it improves learning; reducing them is not: a practice round never gets shorter.

## How the teaching works

- Mastery per module, a placement check, quick checks to skip what a student knows, and memory checks that revisit mastered modules over time (spaced repetition).
- Large question banks: no practice round ever repeats a question (questionKey in logic.mjs), every module holds at least its round length plus two different questions, every round is full length, and the rules tests enforce all three. Every answer must be taught in its lesson (tools/untaught.mjs).
- Wonder questions: one for every two modules. Themes are philosophy, fallibilism (we can be wrong, and that is how we learn), emotional resilience and the reframing of failure. Each is answered by four voices, the core cast by name: Frederick (The Scientist), Chloe (The Artist), Georgette (The Grandparent of Faith) and Mike (The Skeptic), the name first and the label small above what each says (pass LY). After a failed module or several misses, failure and tough-feelings questions come first. No student sees the same question more than twice, and the pools must hold enough for years. Early learners hear them aloud and tap an answer.
- Stories: one short story for every module and one long story for every course. Short stories run 100 to 200 words with five pictures for early learners, 240 to 350 words with six pictures from grade 3; long stories run 400 to 460 words in eight paragraphs with six pictures. They follow the Miniature Gladwell Method: a specific person with a concrete problem, a friction point common sense cannot solve, the concept as the tool, and a zoom out to a universal truth. Write with varied sentence lengths, short ones that hit and long ones that carry, in an active, conversational voice, with one sensory detail and the "so what?" test.
- The cast grows up with the learner. Four core characters mature across the grades into their ways of thinking: Frederick the scientist, Chloe the artist, Georgette the grandparent of faith, and Mike the skeptic and fallibilist. Three side characters appear at random: Savanah, Mike's wife; and their children Jaxon and Harlow. Never bunch stories about the same character together; mix in many one-off characters; keep cultural sensitivity. docs/CHARACTERS.md holds their descriptions. The Wonder voices are this cast speaking as grown people, and the stories are flashbacks to how each came to see the world that way (Mikey, pass LZ: "It's one of the beautifully unique aspects of this app"). Stories never show students in romance.
- Games: a new kind of game for every course, for variety (docs/GAMES-PLAN.md). Since pass MP every game opens on a spoken welcome while it plays itself to show how, then Begin, and a new game is built with its own (Mikey: "Any new games would then be edited tp match these rules as they are created.").
- Keep growing, pass after pass: science projects, life skills and reading lists, wherever they fit.
- Thinkers Mikey enjoys: Socrates, Aristotle, Marcus Aurelius, Viktor Frankl, Arthur C. Brooks, Naval Ravikant, David Deutsch, Karl Popper, the Dalai Lama, Brett Hall, Michael Singer (The Untethered Soul), Rick Hanson (Hardwiring Happiness) and Chris Williamson (the Modern Wisdom podcast). Let their mindsets, outlooks and research steer Wonder questions, stories and lessons. Quoting is optional; any quote must be exact, checked and credited, and no one's words are ever invented. Their books sit on the reading lists where they fit (pass HY).
- Arthur Brooks matters most to Mikey, and two of his ideas are worth carrying into the work. Boredom: Brooks argues that boredom switches on the brain's default mode network, which turns the mind toward questions of meaning and purpose, so filling every gap with a phone crowds meaning out; his practice is phone-free stretches, none in the bedroom and none in the first hour after waking. Hemispheric lateralization, drawn from the neuroscientist Iain McGilchrist: the left hemisphere handles complicated how questions, the right handles complex why questions of meaning, and Brooks argues phones pull people toward left-hemisphere living. Teach both as Brooks's and McGilchrist's arguments, framed honestly: the hemispheres do specialize in some functions, but the popular idea of left-brained and right-brained people is not supported by the evidence, and McGilchrist's larger thesis is debated among neuroscientists.

## House rules

No em dashes anywhere a user can see; American spelling; questions in natural, complete sentences with the context they need (no length limit since pass JE); sentences as long as the thought needs (a 45-word run-on guard; eighteen words in early-years stories, for pre-readers listening) and key ideas 28 words a sentence or fewer; lessons from grade 3 up explain in connected prose with context, worked examples and more than one angle, never one fact per line (docs/FABLE-REVIEW.md); young learners' lessons stay short and demonstrate rather than describe; colons rare and semicolons sparing; clean, readable, secure code with a plain-English header comment on every block; when Mikey supplies wording, use it exactly.
