## I Social and Emotional Development Domain
## I.A Self-Concept
I.A.1	is building competence in controlling own body movements.
I.A.2	can identify own physical attributes and indicate some likes and dislikes when prompted.
I.A.3	begins to show awareness of own abilities.
I.A.4	shows initiative in trying new activities but may not persist when obstacles or challenges arise.
## I.B Self-Regulation
## I.B.1 Behavior Control
I.B.1.a	follows simple rules and routines when assisted by adults.
I.B.1.b	takes care of and manages classroom materials with adult assistance.
I.B.1.c	manages own behavior with adult guidance and assistance.
## I.B.2 Emotional Control
I.B.2.a	recognizes and expresses a range of emotions.
I.B.2.b	is familiar with basic feeling words (e.g., happy, sad, mad, scared).
I.B.2.c	manages intensity of emotions with adult assistance.
## I.B.3 Control of Attention
I.B.3.a	focuses attention on one task at a time but may not stay with it to completion.
I.B.3.b	remains focused on engaging, teacher-led group activities for up to 10–15 minutes at a time.
## I.C Relationships with Others
I.C.1	forms positive relationships with adults and peers.
I.C.2	assumes roles and responsibilities as part of the classroom community with adult assistance.
I.C.3	shows interest in peer play but may be less skilled in initiating and joining a group.
I.C.4	enjoys parallel and associative play with peers.
I.C.5	seeks adult help when experiencing conflicts with another child.
I.C.6	responds with concern when a child or adult is distressed.
I.C.7	interacts with peers and may have preferred friends.
## I.D Social Awareness
I.D.1	shows interest in other people and their feelings.
## II Emergent Literacy: Language and Communication Domain
## II.A Listening Comprehension
II.A.1	responds to situations in ways that demonstrate an understanding of what has been communicated.
II.A.2	shows understanding by following two-step verbal directions.
## II.B Speaking (Conversation)
II.B.1	uses language to communicate basic needs and wants.
II.B.2	begins to use appropriate language, style, and nonverbal cues during communication with familiar adults and peers.
II.B.3	is able to communicate basic information in familiar social settings.
II.B.4	begins to use appropriate language for different situations.
## II.C Articulation
II.C.1	Child's speech is understood by familiar adults and peers.
II.C.2	begins to mimic intonation of language through songs and fingerplay.
## II.D Vocabulary
II.D.1	understands (receptive) and uses (expressive) expected words to label and describe common objects, people, places, actions, and events.
II.D.2	understands (receptive) the instructional language of the classroom.
II.D.3	shows a steady increase in understanding (receptive) and using (expressive) language learned from books, conversations, and play.
## II.E Sentences and Structure
II.E.1	typically uses simple sentences of three to four words, usually in correct word order.
II.E.2	begins to use regular plurals and regular past tense correctly.
II.E.3	uses simple sentence structures with at least one idea.
II.E.4	understands increasingly longer sentences that combine two ideas.
## III Emergent Literacy: Reading Domain
## III.A Motivation to Read
III.A.1	demonstrates an interest in pictures, text, and stories read aloud.
III.A.2	tells a story by looking at pictures or from memory.
III.A.3	notices and connects meaning to environmental print.
## III.B Phonological Awareness
III.B.1	recognizes when a word in a spoken sentence is changed.
III.B.2	begins to distinguish differences between similar-sounding words.
III.B.3	recognizes the individual words in a compound word.
III.B.5	participates in oral syllabication activities.
III.B.6	uses rhyming words through playful activities such as songs, nursery rhymes, and fingerplay.
III.B.7	uses alliteration through playful activities such as songs and read alouds.
III.B.8	participates in onset-rime blending activities (in English only).
## III.C Alphabet Knowledge
III.C.1	shows awareness of letters by singing alphabet songs and recognizing some frequently encountered letters (e.g., first letter of name or letters in environmental print).
III.C.2	identifies the letter associated with the sound of the first letter of his name.
III.C.3	produces the correct sound for the first letter of his name.
## III.D Comprehension of Text
III.D.1	re-enacts a story after it has been read aloud.
III.D.2	makes personal connections to books read aloud.
III.D.3	asks and answers age-appropriate questions about a book.
III.D.4	attempts to make predictions by looking at the cover of a book or the pictures within a story.
## III.E Concepts of Print
III.E.1	can distinguish between pictures and print.
III.E.2	handles books with increasing skill and imitates reading with awareness of directionality (e.g., imitates reading text across a page).
III.E.3	begins to notice the basic features of print (e.g., repeating words, space between words, punctuation vs. letters).
## IV Emergent Literacy: Writing Domain
## IV.A Motivation to Write
IV.A.1	engages in free-choice drawing and writing activities.
IV.A.2	attempts to draw and write for many purposes and begins to explore different writing tools.
## IV.B Writing as a Process
IV.B.1	discusses and contributes ideas for drafts composed in whole/small group writing activities with adult prompting.
IV.B.2	observes and discusses ideas for revising (add, take out, change) drawings and/or written words in whole/small group writing activities.
IV.B.3	shares written products with others.
## IV.C Conventions in Writing
IV.C.1	attempts to write some letters of own name (usually beginning with the first letter).
IV.C.2	uses drawings, scribbles, and mock letters to communicate ideas.
IV.C.3	begins to write using letter-like forms.
IV.C.4	begins to show understanding of directionality (e.g., attempts to write top to bottom, left to right), but may still start at random places on a page.
## V Mathematics Domain
## V.A Number Sense
V.A.1	rote counts from 1 to 10.
V.A.2	counts up to 5 objects with one-to-one correspondence.
V.A.3	counts up to 5 items and demonstrates cardinality by communicating that the last number indicates how many items are in the set.
V.A.4	instantly recognizes the quantity of up to 3 objects without counting (subitizes).
V.A.5	recognizes numerals 0-5.
V.A.6	represents quantities up to 5.
V.A.8	compares sets of objects up to 5 using comparative language (e.g., more than, less than, same number of).
## V.B Joining and Separating
V.B.1	uses objects to demonstrate that adding one or more objects to a set will increase the number of objects in the set.
V.B.2	uses objects to demonstrate that taking away one or more objects from a set will decrease the number of objects in the set.
## V.C Geometry and Spatial Sense
V.C.1	names and describes common 2D shapes.
V.C.2	attempts to create shapes using materials and/or manipulatives.
V.C.3	begins to use language to describe position of objects.
V.C.4	recognizes common shapes, regardless of size.
## V.D Measurement
V.D.1	understands that lengths of objects can vary and be compared.
V.D.2	begins to recognize capacity based on how much space exists within an object.
V.D.3	understands that weights of objects can vary and be compared.
V.D.4	shows awareness of the passage of time within a day.
## V.E Classification and Patterns
V.E.1	sorts objects that are the same and different.
V.E.2	participates in group activities of collecting data and organizing it into graphic representations.
V.E.3	recognizes and duplicates patterns.

## IX Physical Development Domain
## IX.A Gross Motor Development
IX.A.1	masters basic skills of running, jumping, climbing, and pedaling.
IX.A.2	engages in movement sequences with adult support.
## IX.B Fine Motor Development
IX.B.1	experiments with a variety of fine motor tasks but may lack strength and control.
IX.B.2	shows emerging proficiency in tasks requiring eye-hand coordination.
