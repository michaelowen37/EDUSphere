## II Emergent Literacy: Language and Communication Domain
## II.A Listening Comprehension
II.A.1	shows understanding by responding appropriately to what has been communicated by adults and peers.
II.A.2	shows understanding by following three‐step verbal directions.
## II.B Speaking (Conversation)
II.B.1	uses language for multiple purposes.
II.B.2	engages in conversations in appropriate ways, demonstrating knowledge of verbal and nonverbal conversational rules.
II.B.3	provides appropriate information in various settings.
II.B.4	matches language to social contexts.
## II.C Articulation
II.C.1	’s speech is understood by both familiar and unfamiliar adults and peers.
II.C.2	demonstrates growing understanding of the intonation of language.
## II.D Vocabulary
II.D.1	understands (receptive) and uses (expressive) a wide variety of words to label, describe and make connections among objects, people, places, actions, and events.
II.D.2	understands (receptive) and uses (expressive) the instructional language of the classroom.
II.D.3	consistently understands (receptive) and uses (expressive) new vocabulary acquired through books, conversations, and play.
## II.E Sentences and Structure
II.E.1	typically uses complete sentences of four or more words with age-appropriate grammatical complexity, usually in standard word order.
II.E.2	correctly uses regular and irregular plurals, regular past tense, personal and possessive pronouns, and subject‐verb agreement.
II.E.3	uses sentences that combine multiple phrases or ideas.
II.E.4	uses sentences that provide many details, remains on topic, and clearly communicates intended meaning.
## III Emergent Literacy: Reading Domain
## III.A Motivation to Read
III.A.1	engages in story-related pre‐reading activities.
III.A.2	self‐selects books and other written materials to engage in pre‐reading behaviors.
III.A.3	recognizes that all print carries meaning and serves as a means for communication.
## III.B Phonological Awareness
III.B.1	identifies the individual words in a spoken sentence.
III.B.2	distinguishes differences between similar-sounding words.
III.B.3	uses two familiar base words to form a compound word with pictorial or gestural supports.
III.B.4	manipulates compound words with pictorial or gestural support.
III.B.5	begins to blend and segment syllables in multisyllabic words.
III.B.6	identifies rhyming words.
III.B.7	identifies alliterative words with pictorial support.
III.B.8	identifies a familiar one-syllable word that is segmented by onset and rime (in English only).
III.B.9	blends and segments one-syllable words by phonemes with visual or gestural support.
## III.C Alphabet Knowledge
III.C.1	recognizes and names at least 20 letters (upper- or lower-case letters).
III.C.2	recognizes at least 20 distinct letter-sound correspondences.
III.C.3	produces at least 20 distinct letter-sound correspondences.
## III.D Comprehension of Text
III.D.1	retells or re‐enacts a story with a clear beginning, middle, and end.
III.D.2	uses information learned from books by describing, relating, categorizing, or comparing and contrasting.
III.D.3	asks and responds to questions relevant to the text read aloud.
III.D.4	makes inferences and predictions about a text.
## III.E Concepts of Print
III.E.1	can distinguish between elements of print including letters, words, and pictures.
III.E.2	holds books right side up and demonstrates understanding of print directionality (e.g., knows where a book starts and ends, turns pages, points to words left to right, top to bottom, with correct sweeping).
III.E.3	can identify some conventional features of print that communicate meaning including end punctuation and case.
## IV Emergent Literacy: Writing Domain
## IV.A Motivation to Write
IV.A.1	intentionally uses marks, letters, or symbols to record language and verbally shares meaning.
IV.A.2	independently draws and writes for many purposes to communicate ideas, using a variety of writing tools.
## IV.B Writing as a Process
IV.B.1	discusses and contributes ideas for drafts composed in whole/small group writing activities.
IV.B.2	interacts and provides suggestions for revisions (add, take out, change order) and edits (conventions) in whole/small group writing activities.
IV.B.3	shares and celebrates class-made and individual written products.
## IV.C Conventions in Writing
IV.C.1	writes first name (or nickname) using legible letters in the proper sequence.
IV.C.2	progresses from using scribbles and mock letters to forming letters and letter strings as a way to communicate.
IV.C.3	begins to write familiar words using letter-sound correspondences, often using letters associated with beginning and/or ending sounds to write words.
IV.C.4	uses appropriate directionality when writing (e.g., top to bottom, left to right).
IV.C.5	begins to experiment with punctuation when writing.
