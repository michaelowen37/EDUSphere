## 1. What we are building

The Wise Human (formerly EduSphere), by iECHO, LLC, "Where Knowledge Meets Wisdom": a mastery-based, adaptive learning app from pre-K 3 through college, for homeschool families and microschools, Texas first (the Texas Education Freedom Accounts vendor plan). One web page that works offline; nothing about a child leaves the device. Pre-K and kindergarten are free; later grades will open with signed license codes once licensing is switched on at launch.

The aim is understanding and wisdom, not memorization. Students learn better when they look forward to the material (Mikey's point in pass MM), so the app teaches through lessons that demonstrate, stories that make ideas stick, games whose rules are the lesson, celebrations, and Wonder questions that build judgment and resilience, while multiple examples, spaced repetition, mastery, wrong-answer explanations and educator reports make the learning last. Depth, quality and the student's experience come before speed (Mikey, October 19, 2026: "We don't need to lean too heavily in the 2 hour learning part of the vision; depth, quality of learning and student experience matter more.").

The moat is depth: every subject strand from pre-K to college, standards quoted from their published texts, a recurring cast that grows up with the learner, Wonder questions, a game for every course, large question banks, and an offline, private design. A few lessons are easy to copy; the whole connected system is not.

## 3. The two rules above all others

1. **Accuracy of absolutely everything**, whatever it costs in time or passes. Accuracy of what exists comes before new material. Standards are quoted word for word from published texts read in full; facts are checked against sources; numbers are computed in code; uncertainty is said, never guessed over; the newest research is taught, with older findings kept as history with their dates.
2. **The learner first.** When a house rule would make the learning or the experience worse, do the right thing for the learner, say so, and record the exception. Questions and examples are never reduced.

## 4. How the teaching works, as one system

- **Lesson**: shows before it tells. For a child who cannot read yet, the spoken script is the lesson, with a picture on every line, each picture named in its words. Every answer a question asks is taught first. Since pass MQ a young learner hears the device's voice a little slower, with a short rest between sentences (Mikey: "the speed of the sentences can slow a little bit with small pauses between sentences").
- **Practice round**: large banks, never a repeated question, every answer computed and re-derived by the tests. After an answer, More Info, written bank by bank as each course is reviewed (EARMARKS L10), says why each wrong choice does not fit and gives more right examples. A pre-reader answers by tapping, and since pass MO can follow a spoken direction by tapping pictures in order, each tap numbered. Since pass NG, on a device with no voice, the letters questions show a card for the grown-up first, with the words to read aloud and Ready, so the words and the answers are never on the screen together (Q91 (a)); since pass NH every letters question whose answers are letters, sounds or Yes and No does (Q96 (a)), and since pass NI the seven whose answers are pictures beside a letter too (Q98 (a)), and since pass NS one question that asks no letters question, Print tells us something's "What does this print tell us?", whose answers are words a picture's print can show (I love you under Mom's note), its picture hidden while the card lists the answers and its buttons numbered after Ready (Q122 (a)); where a question asks about a letter shown on the screen, the letter (and its picture) waits until Ready, the card saying only what the voice says without it and telling the grown-up to read the question after Ready (Q97 (a)), and a card whose answers are pictures says their names and, after Ready, to point to each picture and say its name again. There is no Hint button (Q94 (c), read again at grade 2's review): the help is the reason after a wrong answer and More Info. Since pass NN every line of the card after an answer, the reason after a wrong one first, is centered, as the question and the answers above it are.
- **Mastery**: a lesson is passed once, then earns its star on another day; memory checks and the Quick Review (a light look back weeks later) keep it alive. A child who keeps missing loops back to the lesson its ideas build on.
- **Stories**: a short story for every lesson (a pre-reader hears it as the reward for a passed round) and, for each course, a long story at the first celebration (every lesson passed) and a second at mastery (every star), each written as the review reaches the course (EARMARKS L1 and L9). Stories follow the miniature Gladwell method: a specific child, a real problem, a turn where common sense fails and the lesson's idea solves it, and a short moral.
- **The cast**: Frederick (the scientist), Chloe (the artist), Georgette (the grandparent of faith) and Mike (the skeptic) grow up across the grades, with Savanah, Jaxon and Harlow dropping in; one-off children fill the rest. Each person is painted the same in every picture, and since Mikey's answer to Q99 (pass NJ) every new painting in color writes each person's skin in plain words, the core cast's in docs/CHARACTERS.md (Skin); a coloring page, black lines on white, writes none. The Wonder voices are the cast speaking as grown people, and the stories are flashbacks to how each came to see the world.
- **Wonder questions**: one for every two lessons, on philosophy, fallibilism, emotional resilience and reframing failure, answered by the four voices with no answer declared right; failure and hard-feelings questions come first after a rough patch. Since pass NJ every question is on by default (Mikey: "I think I want the wonder questions pre-approved by default too so I don't need to describe to everyone how they need to go in and approve them in advance."), and a school turns off, on the Wonder Questions page, any it would rather skip, one by one or all at once.
- **Games**: every course owns a game that teaches its own idea. From pre-K 3 to grade 2 a course's game opens at its first celebration; from grade 3 up, when every lesson has its star (Mikey's answer to Q45). Since pass MP every game opens on a spoken, conversational welcome while its board plays itself under a pointer in step with the words, keeping clear of the answers the child meets first; then Begin. Skip ends it, the speaker under the game reads the words again, and the welcome plays again only when the game is opened again. A new game is built with its welcome (Mikey: "Any new games would then be edited tp match these rules as they are created.").
- **Educators**: plain-English reports computed from an unchangeable record, Action Items, and every course's standards on a map. An educator's screen counts down before it signs out; since pass NJ a student's screen signs itself out after fifteen quiet minutes with no countdown shown (Mikey: "Maybe we should have students auto-logged out after a period of inactivity kind of like the instructors?"), and a browser opened again later than that starts at the sign-in screen.
- **The Guide** (Q93 (a), planned as a program after the pre-K earmarks, EARMARKS L21): in place of an AI tutor, between lessons one of the four Wonder voices says one short tip chosen by rules from the device's own record (a wrong choice a child keeps picking, a skill missed twice, a return after days away, a streak, a review that is due), every tip written and checked in a pass, with a matching sentence for the educator; no AI writes anything at run time, nothing new is stored and nothing leaves the device.
- Science projects, life skills and reading lists keep growing wherever they fit. Thinkers to draw on, credited and never misquoted: Socrates, Aristotle, Marcus Aurelius, Viktor Frankl, Arthur C. Brooks (who matters most to Mikey), Naval Ravikant, David Deutsch, Karl Popper, the Dalai Lama, Brett Hall, Michael Singer, Rick Hanson and Chris Williamson.

## 5. Standards

Texas first (TEKS from the Texas Administrative Code or TEA; the Texas Prekindergarten Guidelines, quoted since pass MR from the 2027 Pre-K Guidelines Review Committee's recommendations, which Mikey adopted before TEA did: "This app will take years to develop so we might as well adopt the new stuff now.", with the 2022 text kept as the record), with national frameworks beside them: Head Start's Early Learning Outcomes Framework, Common Core, the Council for Economic Education, the National Health Education Standards, AFNR, CSTA, the National Core Arts Standards, the College and Career Readiness Standards and Texas's ACGM for college courses. Read a standard in full in the agency's own text before citing it, quote it word for word (the pre-K texts are kept in docs/standards, and a test holds every pre-K quote to them), check the edition, cite a code taught in part as in part and say what waits, and log every expectation no lesson teaches yet in docs/EARMARKS.md. Where an outcome asks that a child "understands (receptive) and uses (expressive)" words, a lesson teaches and cites the receptive half, and the expressive half, the child's own talk, is a grown-up's or the classroom's (pass MQ). TEA is revising the Prekindergarten Guidelines (recommendations out for public feedback in September 2026); when TEA adopts the revision, every pre-K citation is read again (EARMARKS A9 and L17).

## 9. How we keep it right (adopted in pass MM)

Effort goes where it holds most, top first:

1. **Structure**: one source of truth for every fact (logic decides, screens only draw; one list, never two copies kept in step by hand), so the easy change is the right change.
2. **Checks**: check.sh runs every test and the tools the tests call; the comparison tools, the ledgers' generators and the full game layout audit run on their own. A rule a machine can see becomes a test or a tool, proven failing on a real past mistake and its near misses; a mistake class seen twice gets a check (docs/MISTAKE-CLASSES.md ranks the kinds of mistake the checks have found).
3. **Prose**: CLAUDE.md, RULES-AUDIT and the briefs carry only what needs judgment, kept short and current; history is archived, never deleted.
4. **Review**: independent agents in layers (specialists, the alignment read, the final read, second reads of every fix), and Mikey last, who alone approves.

The idea behind it, from the paper Mikey shared (Gu, Kemeny, Yelin and Lukin, October 2026): a model proposes, and an independent evaluator checks the complete final state.
